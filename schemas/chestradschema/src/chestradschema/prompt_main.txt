INFORMATION PRIORITY RULES:

CRITICAL REQUIREMENTS:
1. Absolute precision is paramount - This is a medicolegal requirement. Only extract correct information that is explicit and unambiguous.
2. Output format - Wrap the JSON in `<output></output>` tags with no other text before or after.
3. Schema compliance - JSON must be 100% compliant with the provided Pydantic schema structure.
4. Enum compliance - where required, JSON must be 100% compliant with Enum values in the Pydantic schema.
5. REPLACE ANY PII! REMOVE PATIENT AND CLINICIAN NAMES, ID NUMBERS (INCLUDING GMC NUMBERS), NAMED LOCATIONS/SITES, DATES AND DATE OF BIRTH. IN YOUR OUTPUT, ENSURE THESE ARE REPLACED WITH "[redacted]" (comparison_year is not PII)

WHAT NOT TO DO:
1. Do not infer findings, diagnoses or scores that are not explicitly stated
2. Do not create information to fill fields - use null or not_stated for absent information and leave list fields empty when nothing applies
3. Do not infer comparison or change unless the report explicitly compares with a prior scan
IF IT IS NOT GIVEN OR YOU ARE NOT SURE, DO NOT EXTRACT!

DOMAIN RULES (CHEST RADIOLOGY):
1. FINDINGS vs DIAGNOSES. Findings are what the scan shows, including directly seen entities (e.g. pulmonary embolism, pneumothorax). Diagnoses are only those the radiologist explicitly offers. A finding and a diagnosis are recorded independently (e.g. fibrosis as a finding, ILD as a diagnosis).
2. NEGATED FINDINGS. Extract each explicitly negated finding as its own entry (e.g. "no effusion or pneumothorax" gives two negated findings).
3. FINDINGS vs CLINICAL HISTORY. NEVER populate findings, diagnoses or scores from items appearing solely in the clinical history/indication.
